Rare Birds Nearby

Get started (about 3 minutes)

  1. Get an eBird API key
    Go to ebird.org/api/keygen and sign in with your eBird account (it's free to create one). The string of letters and numbers on the page is your key.
  2. Open the map and paste the key
    Open the map, tap the yellow banner at the top (or Settings), paste the key and press "Save & search". The key is stored only in your own browser.
  3. Pick a location
    Tap "📍 My location", type a city or address into the search box, or simply click the map. Rare birds nearby will appear.

No key yet? You can still look around: the first time you open the map it shows made-up demo data, with a yellow banner where you can paste your key.

The interface switches between English and Chinese with the "中文 / EN" button at the top, or in Settings → Language.

Clicking a dot on the map

  • Click the place name for directions. iPhones and iPads open Apple Maps by default, other devices Google Maps; change it in Settings → Directions with, or use the small "Use …" link next to the name.
  • Click a bird name to open that eBird checklist. Species are sorted by count, highest first.
  • Share this spot: on a phone this opens the share sheet (Messages, LINE, WhatsApp…); on a computer it copies the text.
  • 🏠 May be private: the observer marked the location as private, often a backyard. The data is public, but please check access before visiting and respect the residents.

Handy controls

  • Radius and days: up to 50 km (eBird's limit) and up to 30 days.
  • Look somewhere else: search a place name, or paste coordinates copied from Google Maps (for example 33.45, -112.07).
  • Area: the default is a radius around the center. Switch to "Whole checklist region" to see recent rare birds across an entire county, state, or even country — great for trip planning. It uses the region chosen in "My checklist".
  • On a phone: only the search bar stays at the top; everything else is under the "Filters ▾" button.
  • ☆ Save place: save places you visit often (home, a friend's house, a favorite hotspot) and switch with "★ Saved places".
  • ☰ List: hide the sidebar for a bigger map; drag the divider to resize it.
  • Satellite: switch base maps at the top right of the map — useful for spotting water and habitat.

My checklist

Switch to "My checklist" at the top of the sidebar and choose a region (country, state or province, county). You'll get every species ever reported to eBird there, including migrants and vagrants, grouped by family.

  • Tick the birds you've seen and the progress bar updates.
  • Search by common or scientific name, or show only "Not seen yet" as a to-find list.
  • Ticked birds are no longer marked "Lifer" on the rare bird map.
  • 🎯 Targets: choose this filter to list birds reported in the region during the last 30 days that you haven't seen yet. "Show on map" flies to the most recent sighting. The best way to plan your next outing.

"Lifer" counts real species only: hybrids (A x B) and unidentified birds (sp., A/B) are never marked as lifers, and subspecies or forms (such as Purple Finch (Western)) count toward their species.

Already logging in eBird? Import everything at once
Request your data at ebird.org/downloadMyData. eBird emails you a zip — unzip it and choose MyEBirdData.csv in Settings. Every bird you've seen gets ticked automatically.

Install on your phone

No app store needed — open the map in your phone's browser:

  • iPhone: open it in Safari → Share button → "Add to Home Screen".
  • Android: open it in Chrome → menu at the top right → "Install app" or "Add to Home screen".

Your phone and computer are separate browsers, so paste the key on each. Ticked birds are separate too; to move them, use Settings → Download lifelist.txt and import it on the other device.

FAQ

Can I see bird names in another language?
Use the "Bird names" option: English, or Chinese first. eBird's Chinese names only cover species recorded in Taiwan, so others fall back to English.
"Invalid key"?
Paste it again in Settings, without extra spaces before or after.
The search can't find a place?
Try a nearby town, or right-click the spot in Google Maps, copy the coordinates, and paste them into the search box — that's always exact.
No birds at all nearby?
"Rare" means eBird flagged the sighting as unusual for that place and season, so sometimes there really are very few. Try a larger radius or more days.
Could I lose my ticks?
They're stored in your browser, so clearing browser data removes them. Back up now and then with Settings → Download lifelist.txt, and import it to restore.

Privacy

Your eBird key, location, saved places and ticked birds are stored only in your own browser and never sent to the site owner or anyone else. The site uses your key only to query eBird and iNaturalist directly.
